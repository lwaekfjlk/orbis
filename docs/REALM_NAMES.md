# Realm and city names

Orbis gives countries recognizable names from Western mythological and legendary places, then gives their cities a related naming vocabulary. `src/civilization/realm-names.js` contains 30 source names in five namebases: Norse, Greek, Celtic, Finnish, and Arthurian. `src/civilization/place-names.js` builds fictional settlement names from the same naming culture, a shared realm root, local terrain, and the game's faith traditions.

The source country names are attested in mythology or literature. The cities, patrons, political titles, and distinguishing qualifiers generated by Orbis are game fiction. Saved name metadata records that distinction; city descriptions explain the local naming custom without claiming that every generated city occurs in an ancient text.

## Relationship to Azgaar

The design follows Azgaar Fantasy Map Generator's separation of cultural namebases and political form. Its [namebase data](https://github.com/Azgaar/Fantasy-Map-Generator/blob/master/src/data/name-bases.ts) stores distinct naming vocabularies; its [names generator](https://github.com/Azgaar/Fantasy-Map-Generator/blob/master/src/generators/names-generator.ts) selects through a culture's base; and its [states generator](https://github.com/Azgaar/Fantasy-Map-Generator/blob/master/src/generators/states-generator.ts) separates names from government forms and full titles.

Orbis preserves complete mythological country names so their references remain recognizable. Its city vocabulary is maintained separately and generates related fictional names. A government title such as `Kingdom of` describes the polity; it does not change the name's literary source.

## One naming culture, related cities

A realm retains its naming culture in `c.namingCulture`, separately from its displayed name and `c.nameOrigin`. The culture records `version`, `baseId`, `tradition`, `culturalHome`, `originPeople`, `source`, and `root`. Assignment is seeded from the world and cultural origin. The same fictional people can receive a different literary naming tradition in another world; no species is permanently assigned to Norse, Greek, or another real-world tradition.

The shared `source` identifies a mythological place and `root` supplies a recognizable family resemblance for settlement names. Each namebase also has its own fictional stems and settlement patterns. Examples of the pattern vocabulary include Norse-inspired `-vik` and `-borg`, Greek-inspired `-polis` and `-ion`, Celtic-inspired `Caer`, `Dun`, and `Inver`, Finnish-inspired `-la` and `-linna`, and Arthurian-inspired `-court` and `-wick`. These are a fantasy naming palette, not a reconstruction of historical languages or a guarantee of authentic grammar. The Celtic pool combines Irish and Welsh literary sources for this game; it does not describe them as a single ancient language or religion.

Country-name collisions are resolved within the selected namebase. After its complete source names have been used, qualifiers such as `New` or `Upper` distinguish namesakes. Exhausting a Norse namebase therefore does not silently switch a country's culture to Greek. Qualifiers remain fictional additions to an intact source name.

Naming uses its own deterministic random streams and does not consume the simulation's politics or annual random draws.

## Faith motifs and fictional patrons

Cities share their country's naming culture while drawing devotional details from the naming faith recorded for the settlement. The six motifs are derived from Orbis's existing fictional faiths:

| Game faith | Naming motif |
| --- | --- |
| Dawn Communion | Dawn and sanctuary |
| Veil of Stars | Stars and learning |
| Rootbound Covenant | Groves and ancestors |
| Ancestral Forge | Craft and oaths |
| Tideway | Tides and hospitality |
| Manyfold Tradition | Local shrines and meeting places |

The `Saint X` pattern uses a fictional patron name derived within the naming culture. Related patron names and a shared root let several cities form a recognizable devotional series. The patron is part of the generated world, not an assertion that an ancient Greek, Norse, or Finnish source contains that saint. Other faith motifs use their own settlement patterns; a mythological country name by itself does not determine what every resident worships.

Settlement `p.nameOrigin` records `version`, `baseId`, `tradition`, `realmId`, `faith`, `source`, `root`, `pattern`, optional `patron`, `motif`, `terrain`, `capital`, `generatedName`, and `fictional: true`. These fields describe the naming event, so the interface can explain both the common culture and the settlement's particular name.

## Source corpus

These are names of places in their cited traditions: realms, otherworlds, islands, cities, and fortresses. They are not all ordinary human kingdoms. A source explanation describes the referent rather than claiming a literal etymology or a single universal map of the mythology.

| Namebase | Source names | References and scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Norse | Asgard, Midgard, Vanaheim, Alfheim, Jotunheim, Niflheim, Muspelheim, Nidavellir | Snorri's [*Younger Edda*, Gylfaginning](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/18947/pg18947-images.html) describes the gods' residences and cosmic regions. [*Völuspá*, stanza 37, Bellows translation](https://sacred-texts.com/neu/poe/poe03.htm) places the golden hall of Sindri's kin at Nidavellir, spelled Nithavellir in that translation. The list does not claim to be a canonical enumeration of nine worlds. |
| Greek | Elysium, Hyperborea, Atlantis, Tartarus, Ogygia, Scheria, Aiaia | Homer's *Odyssey* locates the Elysian destination in [book IV](https://classics.mit.edu/Homer/odyssey.4.iv.html), the Phaeacians at Scheria in [book VI](https://classics.mit.edu/Homer/odyssey.6.vi.html), Calypso's Ogygian island in [book VII](https://classics.mit.edu/Homer/odyssey.7.vii.html), and Circe's Aeaean island in [book X](https://classics.mit.edu/Homer/odyssey.10.x.html). Pindar's [*Pythian* 10, lines 29 onward](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.01.0162%3Abook%3DP.%3Apoem%3D10) describes the Hyperboreans' wondrous homeland. Hesiod's [*Theogony*, lines 713 onward](https://www.theoi.com/Text/HesiodTheogony.html) describes Tartarus as a deep enclosure beneath the earth; that place is the naming referent here. |
| Celtic | Annwn, Tir na nOg, Mag Mell, Emain Ablach, Caer Sidi | The [*Mabinogion*, Pwyll branch, Guest translation](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/5160/pg5160-images.html) describes Annwn, spelled Annwvyn there. UCC's scholarly edition and translation of [*The Voyage of Bran*](https://iso.ucc.ie/Immram-brain/Immram-brain-text.html) describes otherworld Emain and Mag Mell. Gregory Toner's [*The Protean Emain*](https://pure.qub.ac.uk/en/publications/the-protean-ememainem-ememain-machaem-ememain-ablachem-avalon-and/) distinguishes Emain Ablach and related names. P. W. Joyce's [*Old Celtic Romances*, “Oisin in Tirnanoge”](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/38041/pg38041-images.html) gives the Land of Youth narrative and identifies his translation sources. Siân Echard's [*The Arthur of the Welsh*](https://sianechard.ca/web-pages/the-arthur-of-the-welsh/) includes a manuscript extract of *Preiddeu Annwn* naming Caer Sidi and discusses its otherworld expedition. |
| Finnish | Kalevala, Pohjola, Tuonela, Tapiola, Ahtola, Vainola | The [*Kalevala*, W. F. Kirby translation, volume II and glossary](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/33089/pg33089-images.html) supplies the epic's lands and domains. Ahtola and Tapiola are the domains of Ahto and Tapio, rather than those gods' personal names. Väinölä is associated with Väinämöinen and also identified with Kalevala in Kirby's glossary; these are literary name choices, not six necessarily separate territories. |
| Arthurian | Avalon, Camelot, Sarras, Corbin | Malory's [*Le Morte Darthur*, Caxton text in the Strachey edition](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/46853/pg46853-images.html) supplies Avalon, Arthur's Camelot, the Grail city Sarras, and Corbin, described as both a castle and a surrounding city. `Corbin` follows the spelling of this edition. These belong to medieval Arthurian and Grail literature, including its Christian setting. The game's fictional saints are separately generated. |

The current corpus selects Western mythological and legendary places. Earlier namebases from other regions and direct deity names are no longer used for new country naming. A historically real city does not qualify merely because it appears in a myth. Likewise, Olympus is a divine mountain dwelling rather than a city or realm and is outside this selection. Some retained place names also have modern geographic namesakes; the stored explanation identifies the literary referent.

## Spelling, saves, and historical continuity

English country labels use conventional spellings without diacritics for compact display and consistent searching: `Tir na nOg` for *Tír na nÓg*, `Vainola` for *Väinölä*, and `Aiaia` for the name also rendered *Aeaea*. Multiword source names remain intact. ASCII spelling is a display convention, not a new etymology.

Loading an existing save preserves its stored country and settlement names, including names from an older corpus. The new naming pass is not a migration that rewrites historical names. Manual country renaming preserves `c.namingCulture`, so changing the visible country label does not erase the origin shared with its cities. Explicitly edited settlement names are also retained.

Existing cities keep their historical names after conquest, secession, or changes of state faith; newly acquired territory is not automatically relabeled in the conqueror's naming style. The saved settlement origin continues to describe its naming event. Repeating the normal generation pass preserves names it has already assigned. Starting a new political history creates a new founding timeline: the new countries and their cities receive related names again, while the terrain, settlement locations and populations remain fixed.

For additions, retain an identifiable source-place name, cite the passage or reliable scholarship establishing it as a place, and give a short explanation of that role. Add invented settlement stems and patrons to the fictional city vocabulary, with the existing origin metadata, rather than presenting them as attested mythological geography.

## Validation

Run `npm run test:names` for naming and introduction checks. `npm run test:names-browser` checks the built offline map, multiple Saint towns in one country, both dossiers, mobile display, and save/load preservation. Set `PLAYWRIGHT_MODULE` and `CHROMIUM_PATH` when using an existing browser runtime.
