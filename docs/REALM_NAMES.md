# Realm names

`src/civilization/realm-names.js` supplies 65 recognizable names from ten mythological and literary traditions. Most are places; some are deities used as namesakes. Each entry stores a short explanation of its referent, not a claim about its literal etymology. The origin is retained with the realm so the interface can explain the name.

## Relationship to Azgaar

The design takes inspiration from Azgaar Fantasy Map Generator's separation of naming data and political form:

- [`src/data/name-bases.ts`](https://github.com/Azgaar/Fantasy-Map-Generator/blob/master/src/data/name-bases.ts) stores distinct namebases, including example names and generation constraints.
- [`src/generators/names-generator.ts`](https://github.com/Azgaar/Fantasy-Map-Generator/blob/master/src/generators/names-generator.ts) selects a namebase through `culture.base`. `getBase` generates names with a pseudo-syllable Markov chain; `getState` modifies a root using culture-specific endings.
- [`src/generators/states-generator.ts`](https://github.com/Azgaar/Fantasy-Map-Generator/blob/master/src/generators/states-generator.ts) keeps `name`, `formName`, and `fullName` separate and derives the formal title from the state form.

Telluric borrows that organization. It preserves complete mythological names instead of generating new syllable combinations, because the name should retain an identifiable meaning. The mythological corpus and implementation are maintained here, rather than copied from Azgaar's namebases.

Nearby capitals prefer the same seeded literary namebase. These traditions are naming sources, independent of a realm's people or faith. Name selection is reproducible and consumes neither the politics nor annual simulation random streams. Compared with older engine versions, new founding diplomatic relations can differ because the former naming pass consumed politics draws; loaded saves retain their recorded state. When the names are exhausted, qualifiers such as `New` or `Upper` distinguish namesakes while preserving the source name. Those qualifiers and government titles are fictional additions, not ancient names or translations.

## Corpus and references

These references document the source traditions and representative names. Ancient texts have variant accounts and spellings; the brief in-game explanations deliberately avoid reconstructing a single universal geography.

## Spelling and scope

The English map uses conventional spellings without diacritics for compact labels and consistent searching. Examples include `Tir na nOg` for *Tír na nÓg*, `Vainola` for *Väinölä*, `Ryugu` for *Ryūgū*, and `Aiaia` as a Greek transliteration of the name also written *Aeaea*. Existing multiword source names remain intact.

Explanations identify the source's role: a place, deity, or other mythological namesake. They should not present all entries as historical states, identify fictional people with real cultures, or infer worship from a realm's name. New entries should retain their complete recognizable source and include a brief, supportable explanation.
